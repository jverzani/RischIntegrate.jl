Symbolic integration · 175 problems from Apostol's Calculus

Apostol Integration Comparison

RischIntegrate on the apostol problems in test/test_apostol.jl with five term types (SymEngine, Expr, SimpleExpressions, SymbolicUtils and Giac.jl), SymbolicIntegration.jl's Risch method on Symbolics terms, and, for reference, Giac's own integrate. Each answer was also checked by comparing a numerical derivative of the answer with the integrand.

What stands out

RischIntegrate solves all 175 with Expr, every answer verified, and 174 with SimpleExpressions and SymbolicUtils, 175 (170 verified) with SymEngine and 172 (170 verified) with Giac. Since the first run (163 solved), it gained radicals with parameters (∫ (a² − x²)5/2, ∫ √((x − a)(b − x))), nested radicals (19), cube roots (21), logs of parameters (∫ xⁿ log(ax), 62), quadratic residues over a parameter field (∫ 1/(a + bx²), 90; ∫ 1/(1 + a cos x), 141) and ₁F₁ answers (∫ 1/log(t)1+n, 172).

SymEngine and Giac.jl have no methods for expinti or li2: RischIntegrate integrates such problems through Expr and keeps the special-function terms, evaluated numerically. With a parameter in such a term (159, 162, and 172's ₁F₁ on SymEngine) the answer cannot be evaluated at a number, so it is counted as solved but not checked.

The two SymEngine answers marked as differing (83, 84: ∫ asec x and ∫ acsc x) are right. The check uses x = 0.31 and 0.57, outside |x| ≥ 1, where the functions are complex; there SymEngine's asec takes the conjugate of Julia's branch, so the derivatives do not match. At x = ±1.5 and ±2.5 they do. The same answers on the other term types are evaluated with Julia's functions and pass.

Giac terms miss 83 and 84: Giac rewrites asec(x) and acsc(x) as acos(1/x) and asin(1/x), and RischIntegrate supports inverse trigonometric functions of linear arguments only. They also miss 172: the ₁F₁ answer needs a symbolic _₁F₁ term, which Giac terms (and SimpleExpressions) cannot build.

Giac's own integrate solves 172, in a third of a second in all. It does not do ∫ log t/(1 + t) (156), which RischIntegrate gives with li2, or the two elliptic integrals 41 and 175 (RischIntegrate gives them with ₂F₁). Every answer it gives checks out except 172, whose igamma Giac evaluates to undef, so it is not checked. (Values are rounded to 12 digits before Giac evaluates them: Giac misreads a 17-digit float such as 0.5700099999999999 and returns undef or a wrong value.)

SymbolicUtils misses problem 165, ∫ eᵗ log(1 + t) dt, with NotElementary; the other term types return eᵗ log(1 + t) − e⁻¹ Ei(1 + t). This has been seen before as depending on the Julia version (this run: Julia 1.13).

SymbolicIntegration's RischMethod solves 97, all verified, and returns the other 78 unevaluated. Every problem it solves, RischIntegrate solves too.

Times are 32–52 s of integration from a cold start, except SimpleExpressions (62 s, 112 s wall: it encodes each expression's structure in its type, so each new shape compiles anew).

How this was run

Solved means the method returned a result with no unevaluated ∫ (RischIntegrate throws when it does not integrate; SymbolicIntegration returns the integral unevaluated). An answer is verified when the central difference of the answer, at x = 0.31 and x = 0.57 with every parameter given a fixed value, matches the integrand to 1e-4. Answers that contain functions the term type cannot evaluate numerically are counted as solved but not checked; such an answer may still be wrong.

Each method ran in its own Julia process, one after another, with a limit of s per problem; after a timeout the process was restarted at the next problem. Integration time is the sum of the per-problem times from a cold start, so it includes compiling. Load time is the time to load the packages.
